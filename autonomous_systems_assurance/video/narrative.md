# Narrative seed

Working title options, all provisional:

- **What Would It Take to Trust an AI Pilot?**
- **The Safety Case for a Machine That Learned to Drive**
- **When the Self-Driving Car Takes Off**

## Hook sketch — approximately the first 20 seconds

“This car has a model of the world. But what if the thing it needs to avoid is missing from that model? To trust the next move, we need evidence about what it sees, what it decides, and what can still recover when it is wrong.” [C-015] [C-016]

Show the architecture by the end of the hook. Start with an illustrative car, not an assertion about a named manufacturer's failure. The picture changes from street to representation to action to the missing observation.

## Sequence

1. What the car thinks it sees; introduce evidence obligations immediately. [C-010] [C-015]
2. The invisible hazard: trace a perception error through planning and control. [C-016]
3. Three possible architectures; explain what a monitor must know and what fallback can still achieve. [C-009] [C-015]
4. Looking inside and making models smaller: useful tools, different evidence. [C-011] [C-012]
5. What permission to operate means; use Cybercab as a dated example of an open compliance inquiry. [C-005] [C-006]
6. The car takes off. Keep the architectural diagram and change the conditions one at a time. Introduce GA-ASI and Zipline with precise approval scope. [C-001] [C-002] [C-003] [C-004]
7. End on the remaining engineering question: which observation or guarantee would let this system take the next action? Point to the worked case while it is still on screen. [C-015] [C-016]

The compiler and wiki live behind the work. Give viewers the argument and its inspectable evidence; do not spend this video reteaching the previous compiler concept.
