# Crosswalk: existing assurance methods into the worked evidence graph

This crosswalk is a design comparison for the hypothetical road-to-air case. It does not claim that any method approves a real trained model, that military guidance transfers to civil aviation, or that proposed EASA material is final. Its purpose is to expose which evidence contract each method supplies, what remains external to it, and which graph node must remain open.

The same comparison is maintained as executable [`method_crosswalk.json`](method_crosswalk.json). Its typed method and mapping nodes are included in the review packages of their linked claims.

AMLAS is the baseline for the **learned component**: its six stages cover ML safety-assurance scoping, requirements, data management, model learning, model verification and deployment. AMLAS says that it is limited to the ML component, should not be used in isolation, and focuses primarily on offline supervised learning. [C-CHAL-006]

MAA/RN/2025/04 is a current but case-specific UK military path. It calls for applicant/MAA engagement and retains architectural mitigation where a prescriptive solution is not available. [C-CHAL-001] [C-CHAL-002] EASA NPA 2025-07(B) is a detailed proposed civil framework, not final material; it includes a direct high-consequence scope limit that the flight-transfer case must preserve. [C-CHAL-003] [C-CHAL-004]

## Crosswalk conclusion

The project contributes a machine-checkable **composition** of these structures: a model-release node, ODD assumptions, hazard nodes, open obligations, evidence-artifact records, exact claim links and downstream paper/video impact. It does not replace AMLAS, prescribe MAA evidence, or turn a proposed EASA framework into an approval basis. The current hypothetical release is intentionally blocked until every open assumption, planned evidence artifact and obligation is resolved.
