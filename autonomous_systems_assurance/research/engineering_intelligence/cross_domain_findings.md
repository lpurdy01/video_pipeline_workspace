# Engineering intelligence: cross-domain limit investigation

Status: research synthesis, updated 2026-09-28 (America/Phoenix). This does not modify the assurance whitepaper or assert a certification result. Original source snapshots and raw Gemini output are under ignored `out/`; source metadata and observation IDs are in `interactive/src/data/`.

## The proposed object of study

For a specified task and operating domain, sensor representation R, training-evidence allowance D, deployment compute budget C, and parameter budget P, define **the attainable frontier**

`F*(P,C | task,R,D) = sup{fitness(m): m is an allowed frozen model with params(m) <= P and deployment work(m) <= C}`.

The supremum is a hypothesis target, not something any of our datasets directly measures. Because the allowed set is nested in P, this definition is nondecreasing in P. Enlarging P can raise the ceiling; it need not do so in every finite range. A release has an achieved score `F(m) <= F*`. Its lower-tail slice score is separate. The same architecture may occupy very different positions below the ceiling because of data, recipe, precision or implementation.

**Limit test:** for targets on named ordinary and tail slices, seek the least `(P,C)` that could reach both, while satisfying observation and deadline constraints. Published successes give lower bounds on F* and upper bounds on the *smallest observed successful budget*. Published failures, or a missing small model, do **not** give an upper bound on F* and cannot establish impossibility. A genuine impossible-region claim needs a theorem under explicit task/model assumptions, or a clearly uncertain empirical extrapolation.

The current site has one illustrative formula for slice error, `e_s(P,n) ≈ e0_s + A_s(P/P0)^(-alpha_s) + B_s(n_s/n0)^(-beta_s)`, to be fitted to the **best attained** models in a controlled family. The coefficients, exponents and floor are unknown. They must not be fitted to a mixed collection of architectures and benchmarks as though there were one law.

## What the parameter-budget atlas currently constrains

The smallest listed ImageNet classification configurations reaching seven top-1 thresholds are: 70% at 3.2M parameters (MNASNet0.75 V1); 75% at 4.3M (RegNet-Y 400MF V2); 80% at 9.1M (EfficientNet-B2 V1); 82% at 12.2M (EfficientNet-B3 V1); 84% at 21.5M (EfficientNet-V2-S V1); 86% at 83.6M (RegNet-Y 16GF SWAG E2E V1); and 88% at 305.2M (ViT-L/16 SWAG E2E V1). These are directly extracted from [TorchVision 0.17](https://docs.pytorch.org/vision/0.17/models.html), and each weight has an individual link in the atlas. The SWAG results use different pretraining, so the sequence is **not** a fixed-data scaling curve. For the broad allowed set represented by the table, each success provides an upper bound on the smallest budget *known to work*, while the best score attained at each budget lower-bounds the unknown achievable ceiling. It cannot establish the budget below which the task is impossible.

A limited within-family comparison is ResNet V1 at 11.7M/69.758%, 21.8M/73.314%, 25.6M/76.13%, 44.5M/77.374% and 60.2M/78.312%, with GFLOPs increasing from 1.81 to 11.51. It suggests diminishing attained accuracy gains in this five-point series, but the shape of a best-achievable frontier beyond those training recipes remains unknown.

## Exact separation of limits and attainment

For any declared task distribution and loss, define `Rraw = inf_g E[loss(g(X),Y)]`, `Rrepr = inf_g E[loss(g(R(X)),Y)]`, `Rbudget = inf_{f in allowed(P,C,R)} E[loss(f(R(X)),Y)]`, and `Rrelease` as the risk of the particular deployed model. If those hypothesis sets are nested, `Rraw <= Rrepr <= Rbudget <= Rrelease`, and the following is an exact telescoping identity:

`Rrelease = Rraw + (Rrepr - Rraw) + (Rbudget - Rrepr) + (Rrelease - Rbudget)`.

These terms separate Bayes uncertainty, representation loss, model/compute limitation, and attainment gap **without** treating hardware as an additive error or assuming an orthogonal decomposition for an arbitrary neural model class. It is still difficult to *measure* the middle infima. Special-case approximation theorems, such as [Yarotsky 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.01145) or [Safran and Shamir 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/safran17a.html), show what a genuine class-conditional lower bound looks like, but do not give a driving parameter minimum.

## Assembly hypothesis that can be tested

Raw pixel complexity cannot be the predictor: keep identical images and change the label/action rule, and the task changes without altering the image bytes. The candidate descriptor is a **task-conditional reusable grammar** under a declared primitive vocabulary, with (a) the description cost of reusable mechanisms, (b) residual/exception cost, (c) effective frequency of each mechanism in independent training situations, and (d) observation geometry at the sensor/tokenizer boundary. This is an extrapolation inspired by [assembly theory](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06600-9) and [skill-frequency scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13506), not a cited law of neural model size. Compare against task-aware MDL, ordinary compression, dataset dimension and pilot-only scaling fits. If the assembly descriptor does not improve held-out frontier prediction, discard it.

The needed experiment is a crossed model-size × training-coverage × tokenization × task-rule sweep, with multiple seeds/recipes at each budget. Estimate an upper *attained* envelope and lower-tail slice distribution, then predict held-out budgets. The data here expose candidate contrasts, but not enough controlled sweeps to calibrate the limit model. A finite-sample confidence bound on the best tested recipe is still not a mathematical upper bound on all possible models.

## Engineering use and next measurements

For a proposed pipeline, compute sensor/token throughput from actual preprocessing, measure target size and optical contrast at required range, then profile each stage on the target hardware. `sum(rate_i × work_i) <= effective throughput` and `memory >= weights + activations + state + runtime` are necessary resource checks. End-to-end deadlines need measured p99/p99.9 latency and allocated capture-to-action timing. The site's workload pane uses published GFLOPs as a compute floor only. It does not compare those floating-point work counts directly with sparse INT8 TOPS.

Next empirical work: (1) capture several within-family model-size sweeps on a single visual task with controlled training recipes; (2) obtain public measured DAA model size, range, slice error and latency together; (3) inspect vendor or third-party AI4 effective throughput with precision and scheduling metadata if available; (4) calibrate a small-task formal bound where target-rule class size is known. Until then, the site reports attained examples and conditional hypotheses, not a universal model-size calculator.
