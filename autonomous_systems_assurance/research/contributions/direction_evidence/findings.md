# Direction-evidence findings — risk boundaries, incident data, and professional qualification

**Research date:** 2026-09-22  
**Scope:** test requested claims about public assurance boundaries, risk numbers, autonomous-road incident reporting, EV comparisons and professional qualification. This contribution does not claim a global standards census.

## Findings

1. **EASA has a much more concrete proposed boundary than “AI is not accepted.”** The retained NPA 2025-07(B) classifies Levels 1A–3A and explicitly leaves Level 3B *reserved*. Its proposed risk matrix uses per-operational-hour likelihood bands, makes every H1/potential-fatality cell unacceptable, and only maps non-unacceptable aggregated scenarios to proposed AL/TQL. It also says quantitative tools supplement qualitative engineering and operational judgement. [C-RISK-006] [C-RISK-007]
2. **This is not a worldwide prohibition.** The document is an NPA, not final material; it has acceptable/moderate cells for some lower-severity/likelihood combinations. The defensible claim is about its proposed scope at publication, not universal organisational willingness to accept AI risk. [C-RISK-003] [C-RISK-007]
3. **NHTSA incident reporting is not normalized safety-rate evidence.** The current SGO dictionary records different ADS/Level-2 reporting arrangements and warns that reporting access affects reporting, data can be incomplete/unverified, one crash can have multiple reports, and public summaries are not normalized. [C-DIR-003]
4. **EV versus ICE needs a denominator.** NHTSA's 2016 quiet-car rule said it lacked powertrain-specific VMT for direct pedestrian/pedalcyclist per-mile comparisons and used a proxy/case-control analysis. This supports a measurement warning, not a current claim about all-crash EV risk. [C-DIR-002]
5. **The maturity/credential causal claim is unproven.** NCEES currently lists more than 20 PE examinations and no machine-learning specialty, but that is one U.S. exam taxonomy. It does not prove that no ML credential exists, that practitioners are unqualified, or that profession maturity explains standards development. [C-DIR-001]

## Editorial consequence

Use a visual **coverage map** rather than an “AI acceptance” meter. Give every source a persistent role/status label. Keep EASA's proposed H1 and Level 3B boundaries on screen, then state the limits in narration. Treat the professional-qualification explanation as an open historical/sociological research question rather than a cause in the whitepaper or video.

## Needed before stronger claims

- A release-grade, current U.S. exposure-normalized EV/ICE crash study with defined outcome, miles, population and confounder controls.
- A structured international credential census before saying that there is no professional ML-engineer certification.
- Historical primary material linking credential/standards-body formation to a threshold of qualified practitioners, if the project wants to make that causal argument.
- A final EASA decision/CRD or successor material before treating NPA 2025-07(B) as final guidance.
