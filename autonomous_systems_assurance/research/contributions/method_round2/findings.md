# Method research contribution: assurance architectures for learned autonomy

Research date: 2026-09-08. Owner: METHOD round 2. All claims in this contribution are provisional and await source-context completion, independent challenge and human disposition. Source records are in [sources.json](sources.json); claim records are in [claims.json](claims.json).

## What the primary material supports

The most practical answer to “how can an unverified learned component participate in a safety-critical system?” is architectural. NASA's Simplex/runtime-assurance work places an advanced or untrusted component behind a monitor and retains a trusted recovery controller. NASA's UAS formalization makes the conditions visible: the monitor must detect the problematic behavior early enough for recovery, and the recovery controller must be trusted under a defined model. This is a conditional safety argument, not a way to turn a black box into verified code. [C-METHOD2-001] [C-METHOD2-002] [C-METHOD2-003]

The important boundary is observation. A monitor that sees the same failed perception output, with the same latency and common sensor path, may not detect the failure that matters. NASA's R2U2 description is useful because it treats sensors, software and hardware as monitored system elements and makes timing a stated property. For this project's car encounter, the assurance case should therefore name the observation path, sampling interval, latency budget, recoverable set and the failure modes that defeat observability. [C-METHOD2-004] [C-METHOD2-019]

The aviation regulatory research is moving toward a layered assurance story. The FAA's July 2024 roadmap distinguishes a static, offline-trained “learned AI” implementation from a system that changes through operational learning. It says updated static versions require safety assurance, while operational learning needs assurance built into the environment or learning process. It also keeps responsibility with the designer/developer. EASA Issue 2 similarly frames learning assurance and explainability as guidance topics and extends its discussion to Level 2 human-AI teaming. These are roadmaps and concept guidance, not approvals for an AI flight-critical controller. [C-METHOD2-005] [C-METHOD2-006] [C-METHOD2-007]

EASA's CoDANN and ForMuLA work gives the project a useful lifecycle vocabulary. The W-shaped learning-assurance idea revisits conventional design-assurance activities around data and training. ForMuLA reports that formal methods can address selected stability, robustness and intended-behavior objectives, while explicitly leaving scalability for high-dimensional ML as an open challenge. The honest architecture is therefore a portfolio: formal bounds where available, data and scenario evidence where needed, runtime controls for residual risk, and explicit unresolved obligations. [C-METHOD2-008] [C-METHOD2-009]

Road-vehicle standards are relevant because the worked example begins with perception and avoidance, but their scope must remain bounded. ISO/PAS 8800:2024 publicly describes vehicle-level risk from AI elements and excludes development of AI elements outside the vehicle. ISO 21448:2022 addresses unreasonable risk from functional insufficiencies, including safety-relevant situational awareness from complex sensors and processing algorithms. UL's public UL 4600 Edition 3 explanation describes a goal-based, technology-agnostic safety case that combines analysis, simulation, closed-course and public-road testing. None of these sources is evidence that a particular car is compliant, and none is an aviation approval path. [C-METHOD2-010] [C-METHOD2-011] [C-METHOD2-012]

Formal methods can meet the learned-controller problem at different abstraction levels. VeriSig composes a sigmoid neural network with a plant and turns the result into a hybrid-system reachability problem. Later interval-reachability work constructs inclusion functions and over-approximates reachable sets for nonlinear plants. The resulting evidence can be strong for the stated model and bounds, but activation restrictions, state abstraction, plant fidelity, computational scalability and sensor-to-state assumptions are part of the claim. A “formal proof” label without those boundaries is incomplete. [C-METHOD2-013]

Probabilistic assurance should be recorded as an estimate or bound with its data-generating and transfer assumptions. The stochastic NN-controller risk-verification paper uses trace robustness and risk metrics, and studies a gap between nominal and perturbed systems whose tightness depends on system closeness. That is a promising bridge for a car-to-flight comparison: the physical environment, sensor statistics and dynamics change, so the transfer edge itself needs evidence. A high test score is not a universal probability of safe operation. [C-METHOD2-014]

Interpretability and minimization are supporting evidence classes, not replacement proofs. SINDy shows that sparse regression can recover compact governing equations for selected dynamical systems, but the method depends on measured variables, data quality and candidate basis. DeepMind's ICML study shows that feature visualization can be disconnected from natural-input behavior and gives a theoretical limitation for black-box networks. Anthropic's own large-model report says discovered features cover only a small subset of concepts and that finding representations does not explain how the model uses them. For the project, “inspect the model” should mean a scoped, testable obligation: identify which feature or circuit is inspected, under which inputs, with what intervention and what safety decision it can support. [C-METHOD2-015] [C-METHOD2-016] [C-METHOD2-017]

## Proposed assurance architecture for the worked example

This is a project proposal, not source-backed certification guidance. Model the autonomous system as six coupled evidence regions:

1. **Operating domain and hazard contract.** State road/air domain, traffic or aircraft encounter geometry, weather, lighting, communications, sensor health, latency and allowed maneuvers. Define hazards as world events and unsafe states, not just model misclassification.
2. **Sensing and state-estimation contract.** For each camera, radar, microphone array or cooperative source, state detection limits, timing, calibration and failure modes. Record what is genuinely independent and what shares power, environment, processing or assumptions.
3. **Learned component contract.** Version the model, training data, preprocessing, output semantics, uncertainty representation and authority. Bind offline retraining to a new assurance package. Treat online learning as a separate architecture with ongoing assurance obligations.
4. **Decision and control contract.** Specify what the learned component may command, what constraints are enforced downstream, and which plant/dynamics model supports those constraints. Use reachability, barrier or statistical evidence at the level the model can support.
5. **Monitor and recovery contract.** Define monitor inputs, sampling interval, worst-case execution time, switching rule, recoverable set, fallback behavior and transition proof. Test omission, commission, shared-cause and stale-state failures.
6. **Operations and change contract.** Track sensor/hardware changes, environment drift, incidents, model updates, fleet evidence and re-entry criteria. Every version should invalidate affected claims and require renewed review.

The architecture has three candidate strategies, which should be compared rather than collapsed into one answer:

| Strategy | What it can establish | Main residual obligation |
|---|---|---|
| Constrain the learned model and formally verify the closed loop | Safety over an explicit model, input set, activation class and horizon | Show model and input abstraction cover the real sensor/plant behavior; manage scalability |
| Place learned behavior inside runtime assurance | Conditional safety if monitor, timing, fallback and recovery assumptions hold | Demonstrate the monitor can observe the relevant failure before the recoverability boundary |
| Use statistical/probabilistic evidence with operational monitoring | Risk estimates or bounds under data, distribution and transfer assumptions | Bound unobserved scenarios, distribution shift and dependence; define action when evidence changes |

The recommended project architecture is a composition of these strategies. A learned perception/planning path may improve capability; a constrained controller and/or reversionary path limits authority; a monitor watches state and timing through an explicitly analyzed observation path; statistical and scenario evidence characterizes residual world uncertainty; the compiler records each obligation and invalidates dependent artifacts when assumptions or versions change. This is the central design hypothesis to test with a road encounter and then with the same diagram carried into flight. [C-METHOD2-018] [C-METHOD2-019]

## Car-to-flight transfer questions

The visual transition from a car to a vehicle taking off should preserve the graph and change named assumptions. The physical plant has different dynamics and stopping/recovery envelopes; the encounter geometry and available maneuver space change; aircraft detection may be noncooperative and intermittent; sensor occlusion and clutter behave differently; and the consequence of a missed detection may be much less recoverable. The method question is not whether “air is harder” in the abstract. It is which contract edge changed, which evidence no longer transfers, and whether the monitor/fallback can still act inside the new recovery envelope.
