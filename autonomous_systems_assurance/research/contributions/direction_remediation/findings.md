# Direction-remediation public baseline

**Research scope:** public FAA, NASA, ISO and NTSB materials inspected 2026-09-25. Copyrighted standards were named only through public regulator, government or standards-body descriptions. No licensed standard text was accessed.

The conventional baseline is an evidence discipline, not a claim that one document or metric proves safety. Public FAA material identifies a system-development-assurance route through requirements validation and design-implementation verification, and an airborne-software route that recognizes the DO-178C/DO-330 document set. NASA's public technical account makes the familiar pillars visible: requirements-based and structural coverage, bidirectional traceability, lifecycle/configuration processes, and tool qualification. [C-BASE-001] [C-BASE-002] [C-BASE-003]

For the road comparison, ISO's public page shows that ISO 26262 spans a functional-safety lifecycle from management and concept work through system, hardware, software, production/operation and ASIL-oriented analyses. It cannot be used to characterize the licensed standard's detailed obligations; SOTIF and AI-specific methods remain separate sources and scopes. [C-BASE-004]

The completed NTSB Tempe investigation is suitable for a bounded worked-case teaching point: sensing/detection is only one part of a hazardous closed-loop chain. NTSB records detection 5.6 seconds before impact and names inadequate safety-risk assessment procedures among the contributing factors. The contribution makes no unsupported claim about the specific classifier, tracker, braking logic, or other vehicles. [C-BASE-005]
