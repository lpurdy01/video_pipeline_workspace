# Integration round 5 — authority/risk coverage boundaries and measurement limits

**Integrated:** 22 September 2026.  
**Inputs:** `research/contributions/direction_evidence/`, retained EASA NPA 2025-07(B), prior risk/standards integrations.

## Accepted, scoped findings

- EASA's proposed DS.AI makes two public boundaries visual: its Table 1 leaves Level 3B reserved, and its Table 2 makes H1 potential-fatality scenarios unacceptable at every proposed likelihood band. The proposed risk process is per operational hour and remains supplemented by qualitative judgement. [C-RISK-006] [C-RISK-007]
- NHTSA's current Standing General Order incident-data dictionary warns that public ADS/ADAS incident data are not normalized and can be incomplete, unverified or duplicated. Its data should not be plotted as a comparative safety rate. [C-DIR-003]
- A NHTSA 2016 EV/hybrid rulemaking documents why a valid powertrain crash comparison needs an exposure denominator and confounder treatment. It is retained as a measurement-limit example, not as a current EV rate. [C-DIR-002]
- NCEES's present PE exam taxonomy contains no ML specialty, but that observation does not support a global claim about ML credentials or a causal claim about professional maturity. [C-DIR-001]

## Rejected or deferred conclusions

- “No organization is willing to accept AI risk” is too broad. EASA's proposed matrix includes acceptable/moderate categories, while its H1 treatment is a proposed, scoped boundary.
- “Level 3B means an approved fully autonomous category with no human in the loop” conflicts with the table's `Reserved / n/a / n/a` text.
- “There is no professional ML engineer certification” needs an international credential census and a definition separating regulated licensure, association certification, vendor credentials and employer qualification.
- An EV-versus-ICE accident number is not added to the paper because the retrieved U.S. authority source establishes a denominator limitation, not a current all-crash comparison suitable for the paper's risk argument.

## Composition result

Added a source-scoped public-assurance coverage figure. The whitepaper and recording script now use it to explain the difference between a component methodology, military guidance, proposed civil material, an authority roadmap and reporting data. The figure is deliberately not a maturity meter, an approval ladder, or a composite risk score.

## Verification consequence

The new sources, claims and reader-facing uses require fresh source-support, challenge and cross-artifact packages. Earlier verdicts are retained as historical records and intentionally do not count on the new input digest.
