# Reader contract: whitepaper

This is the editorial acceptance map for `resource_composition/whitepaper.md`. It is an audience-quality tool, not source evidence or a safety measure.

| Section | Reader question | Concrete anchor | Mechanism | Takeaway | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The question | Why can correct software still make a dangerous decision? | Van, cyclist, and same `clear lane` state | Two physical worlds can be indistinguishable at the planner input. | Observability comes before software correctness. | The cue is hypothetical and does not command the vehicle. |
| 1. Assurance playbook | What established discipline are we trying to preserve? | Familiar plans, requirements, tests, builds and reviews | Conventional assurance links allocation, implementation, verification and change. | ML extends the playbook; it does not replace it. | Public sources characterize standards without licensed clause text. |
| 2. Information problem | What changes when perception cannot see the relevant difference? | Occluded cyclist and Tempe trace | Observation, time, authority and recovery determine whether a response can help. | Change sensing, envelope, authority or recovery—not just the model metric. | The Tempe example is a bounded investigation teaching point. |
| 3. Program lifecycle | What would a team actually produce? | Eight lifecycle artifacts and gates | Each release link gets a named artifact and decision question. | Evaluate the installed release in closed loop and reopen evidence on change. | This is a project proposal, not a standard. |
| 4. Standards landscape | What can standards and authorities contribute today? | AMLAS, MAA, EASA and ED-324 status | Their roles, status and jurisdiction differ. | Use them as scoped inputs; do not substitute titles for case evidence. | Draft/proposed material is dated and not an approval path. |
| 5. Methods | What can testing and analysis tools actually close? | Five-method contribution table | Each method supports one edge of an argument. | Choose a method for a specific failure mechanism. | No listed method is a standalone safety proof. |
| 6. Take-off | Which assumptions change in air? | Bearing-only cue versus usable track and separation | Road and air need different sensing, time and approval arguments. | The interface transfers; evidence and authority do not. | The comparison does not rank domains or authorize flight. |
| 7. Next steps | What can a team do next week? | Seven program actions | A bounded function creates inspectable evidence work. | Start with authority and observability, then control release and change. | The compiler supports the argument; it does not supply evidence. |
