## Why It Matters

AS9100D itself is a licensed quality management standard. These public references can still support the whitepaper's high-level claim that aerospace quality management standardizes organization and supply-chain quality requirements on top of ISO 9001.

## Key Ideas

- IAQG describes 9100 as standardizing quality management system requirements for aviation, space, and defense organizations, usable across supply-chain levels.
- SAE metadata describes AS9100D as including ISO 9001:2015 quality management system requirements plus additional aviation, space, and defense requirements.
- SAE metadata states that AS9100D requirements are complementary to customer, statutory, and regulatory requirements, and that the latter take precedence if there is a conflict.
- NASA notes that it adopted AS9100 into quality policy in 2005 and continued its use through the D revision in 2016.
- NASA's 2024 article says AS9100 content is owned by IAQG and that new revisions are moving toward an IA9100 numbering convention under an IAQG-SAE publication/distribution agreement.

## Useful Claims

- AS9100 is useful as an organizational-quality analogue, not as a software verification standard.
- Aerospace quality systems help frame cross-organization coordination, supply-chain requirements, auditability, and process consistency.
- Licensed standards can still be represented in the project through public metadata, official summaries, and careful citation boundaries.

## Requirements Impact

- The verification compiler should distinguish software-assurance traceability from broader quality-management process evidence.
- Source notes should track licensing limitations and avoid relying on non-public standard text unless the user supplies licensed access.
